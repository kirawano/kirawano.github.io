A hastily-assembled review of The Midnight Gospel

hey

Here's another one that I'd like to write just to get my thoughts in order. Over this vacation I started and finished a show called The Midnight Gospel, a show about exploring spiritualism via podcasts (spacecasts) in simulated realities. I'm writing this because it has been one of the 2 times (Omori is the other one) in which I've full-on cried to.

Emotions and Philosophy

I think that there is an important distinction between a philisophical discussion and an emotional discussion. Sure, there's plenty of philosophy that revolves around catagorizing and channeling emotion, but pure philisophical discussion is always seemingly detatched from emotion, attempting to study it from an advantageous distance. Note here that a philisophical discussion can mean anything from discussing Kant to theoretical physics, so it's a bit vague. Now, what is presented in the Midnight Gospel may seem philisophical, with its discussion of large amounts of (mostly crackpot) spiritual jargon and cyclical undertones, at its core all of the interviews held are emotional.

As somebody who isn't very inclined to belive in spiritual stuff, I wasn't inclined to take what these people were saying very seriously. But I do think that this kind of stuff hold some utility for the people who do believe in them, and the resulting emotions that come from an exploration of human consciousness under a seemingly faulty frame still does hold utility for folks like me.

The climax of the story (when I started crying ;-;) acted as a distillation of the previous bits of emotion that each episode captured. An Eastern spiritual exploration of death that allows a deeply, instinctually, and raw feeling to be unearthed (at least, that's what happened to me). Now, I'm aware that this type of crowd (mainly talking about one of the co-creator's associations with Joe Rogan) aren't great with the whole being politially sound thing, but it's impossible to deny for me how the aesthetics and experiences that this show created spoke to me on a fairly deep level compared to a lot of media today. At least, emotionally speaking; if I'm thinking about this show philisophically (or at least a different headspace than I'm in right now), then a lot of the talking points that each interviewee brought up aren't very scientifically sound and thus don't really have a basis in reality. But that's not really the point, is it?
